Brochure sites lose the map-pack comparison

Most contractor websites look acceptable on desktop but miss what matters when someone searches on a phone with a leaking roof or broken screen. One generic services page, weak geography, no schema, slow builders, and proof buried three scrolls down — that means brand-name searches only, poor mobile conversion, and map-pack clicks that bounce before the estimate form loads.

Lane 1 — Mobile conversion

Homeowners decide on phones — speed and CTAs are not optional

Pinellas and Hillsborough homeowners often compare two or three companies from the map pack before they dial. Estimate-first CTAs only work if the page loads on Slow 4G and the next step is obvious in the first viewport.

  1. Click-to-call and estimate paths Sticky mobile call bars, short forms with job type and ZIP, and clear “what happens next” copy — not a lifestyle collage with the phone number in the footer.
  2. Core Web Vitals as build criteria We treat mobile lab scores as part of the contractor website build — compressed hero media, lean scripts, and hand-coded HTML instead of bloated page builders.
  3. CTAs on every silo Estimate buttons belong on service pages and city pages — not only the contact page. Map-pack sitelinks should land on conversion surfaces.
Lane 2 — Service architecture

Drywall repair does not rank like screen repair or painting

Home service searches combine trade intent and geography. One catch-all Services page cannot capture how Tampa Bay homeowners actually search — or give Google enough structure to match queries to your offers.

  1. Distinct service silos Separate URLs per repair or install intent — handyman silos, enclosure pages, painter interior vs exterior, electrical residential vs commercial.
  2. City pages when justified Geography depth only where you truly mobilize with unique local utility. Thin city spam hurts more than it helps.
  3. FAQ and objection blocks Licensing, deposit, storm response, and timeline answers on-page — with FAQ schema so Search and AI summaries can cite real copy.
Lane 3 — Local SEO & GBP

Website and Google Business Profile must reinforce each other

Map-pack relevance breaks when categories, services, photos, and website URLs tell different stories. The site supports what GBP targets through local wording, service coverage, schema, and internal linking.

  1. Schema from launch LocalBusiness, FAQ, and BreadcrumbList validated in Search Console — not bolted on after a redesign.
  2. Analytics baseline GA4, Clarity, and GSC wired before you scale ads or outreach — so you know which pages earn impressions and which CTAs produce calls.
  3. GBP parity checklist Categories, service list, photos, and website URL aligned so organic and map-pack clicks reinforce the same offer.
Lane 4 — Proof near the ask

Trust signals belong beside estimate CTAs — not on a separate gallery page

Skeptical visitors convert faster when proof sits next to the button: reviews, scope clarity, service area honesty, and before/after context for the job type they searched.

  1. Job galleries per intent Painting, enclosure, handyman, and land-clearing proof paired with the service silo that earned the click — not generic stock photography.
  2. Direct-contact language Licensed, insured, and service-area copy that matches what your team can legally take — especially for emergency and storm-response queries.
  3. Growth systems after the front door CRM outreach, Email-Agent, and referral tracking scale what the website already earns — see home service growth systems when estimate volume justifies ops lanes.

How is this different from a generic contractor template?

Do I need city pages on day one?

Only when you truly cover those metros with unique local utility. Thin city spam hurts more than it helps — we scope geography after coverage is honest.

Can you rebuild an existing Wix or WordPress site?
