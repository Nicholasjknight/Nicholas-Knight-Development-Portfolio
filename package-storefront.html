Good fit, tier choice, and boundaries

Choose based on the transaction you need now, not on a feature list you may never operate.

Good fit

  • Makers and product brands with usable product photos, names, pricing, and fulfillment rules
  • Teams that need one durable page per product instead of a single crowded gallery
  • Early catalogs that can start with inquiry or payment links, then upgrade when order volume proves the need

Why choose the next tier

  • Choose Payment-Link over Preview when buyers must pay now but a shared cart is unnecessary
  • Choose E-Commerce Launch over Payment-Link when buyers combine products, need cart review, or require a managed checkout flow
  • Choose Growth Store over Launch when 21–30 products, richer merchandising, and stronger measurement are part of the fixed launch scope
  • Choose Advanced over Growth Store when subscriptions, custom fulfillment, marketplace sync, data migration, or back-office integrations are essential

Price drivers

  • Number and consistency of products, variants, photos, descriptions, and migration records
  • Cart, tax, shipping, discount, account, inventory, feed, and fulfillment requirements
  • Custom commission builders, calculators, subscriptions, or back-office integrations
  • Content cleanup, policy drafting, analytics events, and launch QA depth

Storefront process

  1. Catalog intakeProducts, variants, pricing, images, policies, payment method, and fulfillment ownership are confirmed.
  2. Conversion architectureWe map category, product, trust, cart or payment-link, and post-purchase paths before implementation.
  3. Technical buildProduct templates, checkout integration, responsive behavior, analytics events, and scoped integrations are implemented.
  4. Measurement and QAWe test product links, totals, forms, checkout handoffs, mobile layouts, metadata, and critical purchase events.

Which storefront segment are you

Product businesses do not all sell the same way. Tier choice tracks how your buyer actually completes a purchase, not just your product count.

Makers and one-off commission brands

Resin, woodwork, custom furniture, art, and build-to-order goods where every unit has variables (size, finish, color) that a rigid cart handles poorly. Preview or Payment-Link Store fits early; a custom quote/commission flow like Mom's Resin Tables fits once volume justifies it.

Packaged product brands

Consistent SKUs, fixed pricing, and repeat buyers — candles, apparel, consumables, small-batch goods. E-Commerce Launch or Growth Store gives you a real cart, tax/shipping rules, and enough product room (20–30 items) to launch without over-scoping day one.

Multi-brand or franchise-style catalogs

Operators running several storefronts, wholesale plus retail, or a catalog that needs inventory sync, subscriptions, or marketplace feeds. Advanced E-Commerce is the scoped lane — deposit-first, architecture before code, because these builds are closer to software than templated pages.

Service brands adding a small shop

Contractors, trades, and local service businesses that want a small merch or parts storefront bolted onto an existing service site. Payment-Link Store usually wins here — no need for a shared cart when the core business is quotes and jobs, not volume retail. Pair it with the relevant home service growth system when the site itself also needs a rebuild.

Storefront FAQs

Do I need a cart on day one?

No. Preview validates the catalog first, Payment-Link Store collects money without a shared cart, and Launch adds full checkout once buyers are combining products.

What counts toward the 20 or 30 product limit?

Each distinct sellable listing at initial setup. Variants (size/color) on one listing do not multiply the count; a true migration of hundreds of SKUs is scoped separately.

Can Advanced start with a deposit?

Yes — $2,500 reserves discovery and architecture. The deposit applies to the approved total once scope, milestones, and terms are agreed.

Is ongoing catalog upkeep included?

No. Storefront tiers are project builds. Ongoing content, inventory, and promotional updates are Monthly Plans or a scoped automation engagement.
