Reading the findings without a developer in the room

The PDF is organized so a non-technical owner can still act on it. The cover carries the run metadata (URL, date, brand). The scores page gives Lighthouse's four category numbers plus a plain-language read on what "Performance 68" or "Accessibility 92" actually means for real visitors — slow load on weak connections, or a missing alt tag a screen-reader user would hit. The findings section is the part meant to hand straight to a developer: specific pages, specific violations, specific header gaps, prioritized rather than dumped in raw tool output.

We deliberately do not rewrite the findings into a sales pitch for more work. If the site is in decent shape, the report says so. If robots.txt is blocking something it should not, or the sitemap is stale, or HSTS/CSP headers are missing, those show up as flagged findings with enough context to fix them — whether you fix them yourself, hand them to your own developer, or come back to us for the work.
